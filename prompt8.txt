## Evidence rules

Every material conclusion must retain its exact project, file, class, method, line, stored-procedure, or SQL-object citations.

Use:

- `[CODE-PROVEN]`
- `[CONFIG-PROVEN]`
- `[RUNTIME-PROVEN]`
- `[INFERRED]`
- `[RUNTIME-VERIFY]`
- `[UNKNOWN]`

Do not convert inferred behavior into fact during consolidation.

Do not invent latency, throughput, database-call counts, production frequency, improvement percentages, or deployment topology.

Recalculate severity rather than copying it automatically from the phase reports. A statically visible loop, large result-set contract, or synchronous wait is a performance risk until runtime impact is measured.

## Required report structure

### 1. Executive summary

Explain in business language:

- how PropelRx is structured;
- the principal scalability risks;
- the confirmed correctness/reliability defects;
- what remains unknown;
- why incremental improvement is appropriate;
- the safest order of work.

### 2. Current architecture

Include:

- all major applications, modules, shared layers, services, workers, reports, and database dependencies;
- verified versus inferred relationships;
- solution/component Mermaid diagram;
- deployment/runtime topology diagram, with uncertain elements marked `verify`.

### 3. Analyzed workflows

Summarize each workflow separately:

1. `MessageOutQueueProcess`
2. RxDetail navigation, locking, and selected-prescription loading
3. `ProcessNewRx/CreateRxs`

For each include:

- trigger;
- end-to-end call path;
- read/write behavior;
- database and external interactions;
- threading/UI behavior;
- transaction boundaries;
- error and recovery behavior;
- symbolic database-call model;
- exact evidence citations;
- runtime-verification requirements.

### 4. Confirmed defects

Keep confirmed defects separate from performance risks.

Include the queue-worker defects:

- `ProcessCommunication(...)` always returns `true`;
- successful Diem communications may not enter the success list;
- `First(...)` followed by an ineffective null check.

For every defect provide:

- exact citation;
- triggering execution path;
- impact;
- effort;
- implementation risk;
- test required;
- rollback approach.

Do not classify the following as confirmed defects without requirements or runtime proof:

- RxDetail N+1-like locking/hydration;
- 67-result-set prescription contract;
- dispatcher-pumping waits;
- ProcessNewRx partial-success behavior;
- retry duplicate risk.

### 5. Scalability and performance risks

Cover:

- queue selection/claim separation;
- conditional multi-worker race;
- potentially abandoned `I` queue records;
- per-operation database round trips;
- per-patient/Rx/caregiver/communication queue chattiness;
- per-Rx lock hydration;
- repeated selected-Rx hydration;
- `RxDataPoint.All` and the multi-result-set payload;
- synchronous service/database work from the UI path;
- selection-triggered loading;
- ProcessNewRx per-item/per-action writes;
- partial-success retry amplification.

For each state:

- mechanism;
- affected workflow;
- evidence;
- confidence;
- runtime measurement needed;
- incremental mitigation.

### 6. Data consistency and reliability

Include:

- queue state transitions;
- queue crash/restart behavior;
- atomic claim uncertainty;
- prescription item transaction boundaries;
- operations outside the core prescription transaction;
- batch partial-success behavior;
- staging, attachment, PIT, OCR, lock, claim, and print consistency;
- idempotency and duplicate-submission uncertainties.

### 7. Database chattiness

Clearly distinguish:

- database round trips;
- stored-procedure executions;
- result sets returned by one execution;
- calls inside loops;
- external network calls.

Do not describe 67 result sets as 67 database round trips.

Include symbolic formulas and their assumptions. Identify nested operations that are not fully expanded.

### 8. Required Mermaid diagrams

Generate and validate:

1. Solution/component architecture.
2. Deployment/runtime topology.
3. `MessageOutQueueProcess` sequence.
4. Queue/worker architecture.
5. Queue database-chattiness map.
6. RxDetail loading sequence.
7. RxDetail UI-loading timeline.
8. ProcessNewRx write sequence.
9. Read-versus-write architecture.
10. Transaction and consistency boundaries.
11. Current versus incremental target architecture.

Keep diagrams readable and split oversized diagrams.

### 9. Top findings table

Use stable IDs and include:

- finding;
- evidence status;
- severity;
- confidence;
- affected workflow;
- precise mechanism;
- citations;
- runtime verification.

### 10. Recommendations table

For every recommendation include:

- linked finding IDs;
- proposed incremental change;
- expected qualitative benefit;
- effort;
- delivery risk;
- prerequisites;
- validation;
- rollout;
- rollback;
- exact change area.

### 11. Quick wins

Limit quick wins to evidence-supported, low-disruption changes such as:

- correcting confirmed queue-worker defects;
- adding workflow correlation and timing;
- recording queue selected/claimed/completed/failed counts;
- measuring queue lag and stale `I` records;
- measuring RxDetail first-render timing and SQL executions;
- exposing ProcessNewRx partial-success outcomes clearly;
- protecting against repeated submission where evidence supports it.

Do not describe major async rewrites, schema changes, or stored-procedure redesigns as quick wins.

### 12. Medium-term incremental changes

Evaluate:

- conditional atomic queue claiming;
- stale in-process recovery;
- targeted queue batching;
- reducing per-Rx lock hydration;
- avoiding redundant selected-Rx reloads;
- deferring secondary RxDetail data;
- retry-safe/idempotent prescription creation;
- improved partial-success reconciliation;
- verified SQL tuning based on Query Store or execution plans.

Preserve the existing Prism UI, service layer, DAO layer, Windows services, and database unless evidence later proves a boundary must change.

### 13. Areas not to change

Explicitly identify:

- stable module composition;
- existing service/DAO contracts without hotspot evidence;
- database objects lacking runtime evidence;
- vendor/integration boundaries;
- business and regulatory logic;
- components outside measured critical paths.

### 14. Runtime-verification plan

Provide executable measurement plans for:

- queue instance count and deployment topology;
- duplicate queue selection;
- stale `I` records;
- queue lag and retry distribution;
- RxDetail SQL executions and time to first meaningful render;
- `p_GetPrescription_MultiRS` duration, reads, result sizes, and plans;
- lock contention;
- ProcessNewRx partial failure followed by retry;
- duplicate prescription/claim/attachment behavior;
- actual adjudication and printing reachability.

Include privacy-safe logging requirements and do not log patient or prescription data.

### 15. Prioritized roadmap

Organize into:

- Stage 0: verify and instrument;
- Stage 1: correct confirmed defects;
- Stage 2: remove proven waste and duplicate work;
- Stage 3: harden queue and prescription consistency;
- Stage 4: optimize verified SQL/UI hotspots;
- Stage 5: remeasure and reassess.

### 16. Incremental target architecture

Show:

- what remains unchanged;
- where instrumentation is added;
- where queue claiming/recovery is hardened;
- where RxDetail loading is reduced or deferred;
- where prescription retries are made safer;
- how deployment can occur workflow by workflow.

### 17. Decision summary

Finish with:

- what CareRx can approve now;
- what requires runtime evidence;
- what should be deferred;
- the first three implementation candidates;
- the first three measurements to establish.

Rewrite everything as one cohesive report. Do not paste phase reports together or preserve contradictions.
