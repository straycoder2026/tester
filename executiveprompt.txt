## Mandatory executive output: issues by delivery horizon

The main output must be tables—not primarily narrative. Categorize every material performance, scalability, reliability, and claim-ordering issue into one of these cumulative horizons:

- **0–2 months:** issues two developers can investigate, measure, test, or fix now.
- **2–6 months:** issues requiring baseline measurements, additional tests, or moderate changes.
- **6–12 months:** larger architectural issues that should be addressed only if earlier evidence and business decisions justify them.

Create a separate table for each horizon. Use these exact columns:

| ID | Issue | Evidence and current impact | Why this horizon? | What should change? | How will it be done? | Effort and dependencies | Business/claim-ordering risk | How success will be measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For every row:

- **Issue:** Name the concrete problem, not a generic technology recommendation.
- **Evidence and current impact:** Cite exact code/SQL locations. Distinguish measured impact from a code-proven risk whose impact is not yet measured.
- **Why this horizon?:** Explain why it belongs in 2, 6, or 12 months given two developers, prerequisites, and business risk.
- **What should change?:** State the desired behavior or outcome in plain language.
- **How will it be done?:** Give the specific incremental implementation approach, affected components, tests, rollout, and rollback.
- **Effort and dependencies:** Estimate developer-days or weeks, plus required QA, DBA, operations, vendor, or CareRx business input.
- **Business/claim-ordering risk:** State whether the change could affect prescription correctness, claims, reversals, rebills, audit, or same-patient claim sequence.
- **How success will be measured:** Specify a baseline and target metric. If no baseline exists, the first deliverable is to establish it; do not invent a target percentage.

Include known issues such as queue-worker defects, non-atomic queue claiming, stale in-process records, queue database chattiness, RxDetail loading and UI waits, ProcessNewRx partial success, and claim-sequencing uncertainty. Add other issues only when supported by code or runtime evidence.

Do not place an issue in the 12-month bracket merely because it sounds architectural. Put it there only when its dependencies and two-developer effort justify that timing. If an issue cannot safely be scheduled, put it in a separate **“Unscheduled—decision or evidence required”** table and state exactly what would allow scheduling.

After the three tables, provide:

1. The top three actions CareRx should authorize now.
2. The decisions or access CareRx must provide.
3. What the two developers will deliver at the end of months 2, 6, and 12.
4. Which changes are conditional rather than committed.
5. The major approaches that should not be pursued and why.

Keep the executive summary to one page. Detailed code traces and diagrams belong in an appendix. The tables must be understandable without reading the appendix.
